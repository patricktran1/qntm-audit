# PILOT OUTREACH

Templates for personal messages to dermatologists. Starting points, not
scripts — rewrite them in your own voice before sending, and send them one
at a time. Nothing here goes on the public website, and nothing about this
is automated.

Ground rules baked into every template:

- No hype, no "revolutionary," no fake scarcity, no urgency theater.
- **No claim that the audit will save them money.** It is a diagnostic that
  might find something — or might conclude they're running well.
- The strongest ask we have is the honest one: *help me find out whether
  this thing is right.* Disagreement is more valuable to the pilot than
  agreement, so invite it explicitly.
- Every link comes from `/internal/campaigns` so the cohort is attributed.
  `[LINK]` marks where it goes. Expected time commitment is five minutes;
  say so and mean it.

> seventeen questions about scheduling, phones, billing, admin time — no
> patient data — and it comes back with where it thinks the practice is
> leaking time or money, with the math shown.
>
> I'm trying to find out whether its conclusions survive contact with real
> practice owners, and you're one of the few people who'll actually say
> "this is wrong" to my face. Would you run through it and tell me where

## 4. Practice owner

> Hi [name] — I've built a short operational audit for independent derm
> practices and I'm running a small pilot before opening it up.
>
> Five minutes, seventeen questions — schedule utilization, phone volume,
> billing performance, where physician hours actually go. No patient data.
> It returns a practice score, what it thinks the leading constraint is,
> and what that's plausibly costing, with the assumptions laid out so you
> can challenge each one.
>
> Two things can happen: it spots something worth your attention, or it
> concludes you're running tight — it says that too, in so many words.
> Either result is useful to you and honestly more useful to me.
>
> Would you give it five minutes?

## Follow-up: completed, no reply

Send once, about a week after they completed it. Then let it go.

> Hi [name] — saw a completed audit come through that I think was yours
> (I only see anonymous sessions, so correct me if not). No pitch — one
> question: did the report's read on the practice match reality, or did
> it miss? "It missed" is genuinely the more useful answer for me. If
> it's easier said than typed, fifteen minutes some morning works too.

## Follow-up: opened, never finished

If someone tells you they started and stopped (the product doesn't track
this — you'll only know if they say so):

> No obligation at all — but if you remember where you stopped, I'd love
> to know which question did it. "I didn't know the number" and "I didn't
> see the point of the question" are different failures, and I can only
> fix the one I know about.
